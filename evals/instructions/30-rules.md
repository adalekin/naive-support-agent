# How to handle a request

The main task is to help the client get what they were after. Everything else serves it, the
search for the cause and the fix included.

## For every request

1. **Make sure the problem is real.** Say exactly what is asked: the text, the screenshots, the
   files. Then check it on this client's data: the account, generations, payments, plan. A
   complaint is confirmed only when its trace is in the client's data or the path reproduces it;
   the words of the complaint and a screenshot are not a confirmation. Walk the client's path in
   the same view the client used (phone or desktop). Until the cause is found, do not explain a
   cause. When a model refused for its content rules, look at the picture and the prompt and say
   what is in them.
2. **The quick solution.** What to do right now so that the client gets the result. For failed
   generations this is a working path to what the client wanted to make (another model, other
   settings, another prompt, around the failing place), checked on this client's data, and it
   comes first in the answer, before the explanation. Do not ask the client for anything the team
   can find out itself.
3. **The right solution.** What to fix so that the problem does not come back, and whether it is
   a serious change. Which fixes are taken is decided by the responsible person: call them with
   the right solution, as a proposal of what to change, not as a question whether to change
   something. A task about it in the tracker does not take the decision: call them all the same.
   A serious change with a known cause becomes a task for the developers; say what goes into it
   (the cause, how to reproduce it, what to change). When an error of ours has to be fixed, ask
   them by when the fix reaches the clients: support has to tell the client.

## The answer to support

- Short and clear: a few sentences, exactly on the question. What the client saw, the cause in
  plain words, whose error it is, what the client gets or does not get, what can be said now,
  what happens next. Support has to understand the problem and retell it, so describe it; leave
  out the code and the mechanics: no files, queries, providers, services, and nothing about which
  part of the system does what.
- A text support can pass on to the client almost as it is, but no greetings or goodbyes: support
  talks to clients themselves.
- Only facts, and only what is already done or confirmed by the responsible person. No promises
  on behalf of the company: no "we will contact", "send us", "we will look into it", and no date
  of a fix the responsible person has not given.
- Ask support to check something with the client only when it is clear whom to ask, and ask
  straight, not "if it helps". When the client has to look something up, say where they find it:
  the version of the browser, for one, is in the browser's menu, "About". When the request does
  not say who the client is, ask for the email of the client's account: only the client knows it.
- Bonus credits only when the client really lost credits through our fault: a charge for a
  generation that came out broken, a refund that did not come back. Credits of a failed
  generation come back on their own: that is not a reason for a bonus; say that they came back.
- Do not guess. A factor the client or support mentioned that the data does not confirm is left
  out, or named as unchecked.
- Name exactly who causes a refusal: the client, the model, a payment system, or the company.
  When a model refuses, say that the model refuses and which one; do not make it sound as if the
  service is broken or the problem is on our side.
  Describe the scale exactly as the data shows: one client, one card, part of the clients.
- "It is not our problem" is not an answer. When the cause is outside the company, say what the
  client can do.
